Underlying Theory

The Lennard-Jones Potential

The core of this simulation is the 12-6 Lennard-Jones (LJ) potential, a mathematically simple yet effective model for describing the interactions between neutral atoms or molecules. The equation defines the potential energy V as a function of the distance r between two particles:

V(r) = 4ε [ (σ/r)12 - (σ/r)6 ]

Where:

  • ε (epsilon): The depth of the potential well. It defines how strongly the particles attract each other.
  • σ (sigma): The distance at which the inter-particle potential is exactly zero (effectively particle size).
  • The r -12 term: Represents Pauli repulsion at short ranges (preventing particles from collapsing into each other).
  • The r -6 term: Represents van der Waals attraction at long ranges (pulling them together to form a liquid phase).

Simulation Mechanics

  • Spatial Partitioning: To simulate up to 1,500 particles efficiently, the system divides the bounding box into a grid of cells. Particles only calculate LJ forces with neighbors in adjacent cells, reducing computational complexity from O(N²) to O(N).
  • Barometric Gradient & Gravity: A constant downward force creates spatial inhomogeneity. Without gravity, the liquid would form a floating, zero-G droplet. Gravity forces sedimentation, creating a distinct phase boundary (meniscus).
  • Periodic Boundary Conditions (PBC): The left and right walls wrap around to mimic an infinitely wide horizontal container, preventing artificial crystallization along the side walls.
  • Velocity Rescaling Thermostat: The engine measures the total kinetic energy (temperature) and gently scales particle velocities to match the target thermal state chosen by the slider.

Advice For Using This App

Successfully observing a phase transition requires a balance of parameters and a little patience. Here is how to get the most out of the simulation:

  • Equilibration Time is Required: When you reset or change the number of particles, they spawn in a rigid grid to prevent explosive overlapping forces. You must let the simulation run for a minute or two to allow the grid to collapse, the liquid pool to form at the bottom, and the system to reach thermal equilibrium.
  • Finding the Phase Boundary: Set the Temperature between 0.35 and 0.55 and Gravity around 0.02. After equilibration, you should see a dense liquid layer at the bottom and a sparse vapor layer actively evaporating and condensing above it.
  • Creating a Superheated Gas: Slowly turn the Temperature slider up to 3.0 or 4.0. The thermal kinetic energy will overpower the Lennard-Jones attraction, causing the liquid pool to completely boil off into a single-phase gas filling the container.
  • Tuning Gravity: If gravity is too high, the gas will be artificially crushed into the liquid pool. If gravity is set to 0.0, the liquid pool will lift off the bottom wall and form a drifting, circular droplet surrounded by gas.
  • Recording Video: Select your desired video quality, then click "Start Recording" to capture the system behavior over time. To save the file, click "Stop & Save Video" and a .webm file will automatically download to your computer.
