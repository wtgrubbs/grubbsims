Underlying Theory: X-Ray Diffraction (XRD)

1. Purpose of X-Ray Diffraction

X-Ray Diffraction (XRD) is a primary analytical technique used to determine the atomic structure, crystallographic phase, and lattice dimensions of crystalline materials. Because the wavelength of X-rays is on the same order of magnitude as atomic spacing (~0.1 nm / 1 Å), crystalline lattices act as three-dimensional diffraction gratings for X-rays.

2. Fundamental Physics: Bragg's Law

When monochromatic X-rays strike a crystalline lattice, secondary waves scattered from adjacent parallel crystal planes constructively interfere when the path length difference is an integer multiple of the X-ray wavelength. This condition is governed by Bragg's Law:

$$ n \lambda = 2 d_{hkl} \sin(\theta) $$
  • $n$: Order of diffraction (typically $n = 1$).
  • $\lambda$: Incident X-ray wavelength (nm or Å).
  • $d_{hkl}$: Interplanar spacing between crystallographic planes $(hkl)$.
  • $\theta$: Scattering angle (Bragg angle). The total deflection of the beam is $2\theta$.

3. Interplanar Spacing for Cubic Crystals

For cubic crystals (where unit cell edge lengths $a = b = c$), the spacing $d$ between parallel planes designated by Miller indices $(h, k, l)$ is directly related to the lattice parameter $a$:

$$ d_{hkl} = \frac{a}{\sqrt{h^2 + k^2 + l^2}} $$

4. Structure Factor & Selection Rules

Not all lattice planes produce observable diffraction reflections. Destructive interference from interior atoms causes systematic absences depending on the cubic crystal lattice symmetry (governed by the Structure Factor $F_{hkl}$):

  • Primitive Cubic (SC): All reflections allowed except where $h^2+k^2+l^2$ cannot be expressed as sum of 3 squares (e.g., 7, 15).
  • Body-Centered Cubic (BCC): Reflection allowed only when $(h + k + l)$ is an EVEN integer.
  • Face-Centered Cubic (FCC): Reflection allowed only when $h, k, l$ are ALL EVEN or ALL ODD (unmixed).

5. Powder Diffraction & 2D Area Detector Pattern

In a powder sample consisting of millions of randomly oriented micro-crystallites, each set of planes $(hkl)$ forms a forward-scattered diffraction cone with a semi-angle of $2\theta$. When these cones intersect a flat 2D area detector located at distance $D$, they form concentric circular rings (Debye-Scherrer rings) with radius $R$:

$$ R = D \cdot \tan(2\theta) $$

By measuring ring radii $R$, scientists compute scattering angles $2\theta$, calculate interplanar spacings $d_{hkl}$, and decipher both the lattice constant $a$ and crystal structure type.
