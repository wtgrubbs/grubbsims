Understanding Data Visualization

App Purpose: This tool helps you visually explore linear relationships across numeric variables. We use heatmaps for quick summaries, but include deeper visualization techniques to prevent misinterpretation of raw numbers.

1. Pearson's Correlation Coefficient ($r$)

Pearson's $r$ measures the strength and direction of a linear relationship between two variables on a scale from -1.0 to +1.0.

$$ r = \frac{\sum (X - \bar{X})(Y - \bar{Y})}{\sqrt{\sum (X - \bar{X})^2 \sum (Y - \bar{Y})^2}} $$
  • +1.0 (Strong Positive): As $X$ increases, $Y$ increases proportionally.
  • 0.0 (No Linear Relationship): No apparent linear pattern.
  • -1.0 (Strong Negative): As $X$ increases, $Y$ decreases proportionally.

2. Interactive Scatterplots (Always look at your data!)

Summary statistics can be deceiving. A high $r$ value might be caused by a strong relationship, or it might be skewed by a single extreme outlier. Click any off-diagonal cell in the heatmap to open a 2D scatterplot of those two variables, complete with a linear regression line.

3. Statistical Significance ($p < 0.05$)

An $r$ value of $0.6$ might look strong, but if your dataset only has 4 rows, it is highly likely to be random noise. By checking the "Mask Insignificant" box, the app tests each correlation against the null hypothesis (that there is zero correlation). If the probability ($p$-value) of the observed correlation occurring by chance is greater than 5% ($p > 0.05$), the cell is crossed out.

4. Univariate Histograms (The Diagonal)

The diagonal of a standard correlation matrix is always $1.0$ (a variable compared to itself). Instead of showing useless dark squares, this application repurposes the diagonal to display Histograms. These mini bar-charts show the distribution of that single variable, allowing you to instantly spot normally distributed data versus skewed data.

CSV Format Requirements

Ensure your CSV file adheres strictly to these guidelines for seamless rendering:

  • Numerical Data Only: The body of your spreadsheet must consist exclusively of quantitative numbers. Text columns will be ignored.
  • Header Row: The very first row must contain text-based column headers.
  • Short Header Names: Keep names short (e.g., Temp, GPA) to prevent label overlaps.
  • Minimum Dimensions: The CSV must include at least 2 numeric columns and 3 rows of data.
  • Clean Data: Empty cells or text like N/A will be safely excluded from calculation pairs.
