Matrix Mechanics Theory

1. Vector Representation ($\vec{v}$)

A 2x2 pocket cube contains 8 corner pieces. Each piece has 3 colored stickers (24 total facelets). We define a 24-element state vector representing these 8 blocks:

$$\vec{v} = [\mathbf{c}_1^T, \mathbf{c}_2^T, \dots, \mathbf{c}_8^T]^T \quad \text{where } \mathbf{c}_i = [s_{i,1}, s_{i,2}, s_{i,3}]^T$$

2. Block Permutation Matrices

Any face turn is a 24×24 sparse matrix made of 8×8 blocks of 3×3 submatrices. Because the 3 colors on a physical corner are locked together, moving a corner applies a $3 \times 3$ permutation matrix $R_\theta$ to its block.

$$P = \begin{bmatrix} A_{1,1} & \dots & A_{1,8} \\ \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ A_{8,1} & \dots & A_{8,8} \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{24 \times 24}$$

Step-by-Step Matrix Example

Applying the $U$ (Up) Face Turn

Let's map out exactly what happens mathematically when you apply a single $U$ move.

1. The Array Mapping

We map the top 4 cubies to the first 4 blocks in our vector $\vec{v}$:

  • $\mathbf{c}_1$: Up-Front-Left (UFL)
  • $\mathbf{c}_2$: Up-Front-Right (UFR)
  • $\mathbf{c}_3$: Up-Back-Right (UBR)
  • $\mathbf{c}_4$: Up-Back-Left (UBL)

2. The Physical Swap

A clockwise $U$ turn shifts the corners in a cycle:

UFL $\rightarrow$ UBL $\rightarrow$ UBR $\rightarrow$ UFR $\rightarrow$ UFL

(Notice that the bottom 4 cubies $\mathbf{c}_5 \dots \mathbf{c}_8$ are completely unchanged.)

3. The Matrix Equation

To express this physical swap algebraically, we construct an $8 \times 8$ block matrix where the top-left $4 \times 4$ quadrant shifts the vectors, and the bottom-right is the identity matrix $I_4$.

$$ \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{c}_1 \\ \mathbf{c}_2 \\ \mathbf{c}_3 \\ \mathbf{c}_4 \\ \hline \mathbf{c}_5 \\ \mathbf{c}_6 \\ \mathbf{c}_7 \\ \mathbf{c}_8 \end{bmatrix}_{\text{new}} = \left[ \begin{array}{cccc|cccc} 0 & 0 & 0 & R_1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ R_2 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & R_3 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & R_4 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ \hline 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & I & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & I & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & I & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & I \end{array} \right] \cdot \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{c}_1 \\ \mathbf{c}_2 \\ \mathbf{c}_3 \\ \mathbf{c}_4 \\ \hline \mathbf{c}_5 \\ \mathbf{c}_6 \\ \mathbf{c}_7 \\ \mathbf{c}_8 \end{bmatrix}_{\text{current}} $$

The $3 \times 3$ submatrices $R_k$ not only move the block into the new position, but they permute the internal elements of $\mathbf{c}_i$ to represent how the physical stickers twist spatially as the corner travels around the cube!

Cube Notation Guide

Absolute Coordinate System

The face rotation buttons map to the cube's absolute 3D space. They do not change based on where you move the camera. If you rotate the camera to look at the back, pressing 'F' (Front) still rotates the side facing away from you!

A letter means a 90° clockwise turn (looking directly at that face). An apostrophe (e.g., U') means 90° counter-clockwise.

U: Up
D: Down
F: Front
B: Back
L: Left
R: Right
